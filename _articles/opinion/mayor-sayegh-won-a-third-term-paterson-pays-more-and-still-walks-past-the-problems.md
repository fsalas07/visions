title: "Mayor Sayegh Won a Third Term. Paterson Pays More and Still Walks Past the Problems."
author: Xavier Santiago
author_role: "Opinions Editor"
date: 2026-11-16T10:00:00.000-05:00
summary: "Andre Sayegh won a third term with fewer votes than ever, while Paterson's tax bills climb and the problems students walk past every day stay unanswered."
---
Andre Sayegh just won a third term as Paterson's mayor with 7,020 votes, and his support keeps shrinking. According to the Passaic County Clerk's official results, he won with 9,047 votes in 2018 and 7,835 in 2022, so his vote count has dropped every election. His 7,020 votes are 33.1% of the votes cast for mayor, his lowest share in any of his three wins, down from 40.8% in 2018 and 48.4% in 2022. By my math, that is also 8.1% of the 87,013 registered voters the county lists, and 4.3% of the 161,793 residents the Census Bureau estimated for 2025. So what are residents getting for what they pay?

They are paying more. According to the New Jersey Division of Taxation, the average residential tax bill in Paterson rose from $9,579 in 2023 to $9,943 in 2024 and $10,358 in 2025, up 8.1% in two years, though that includes school and county taxes. According to Jersey Intel, the proposed 2026 budget raises the municipal levy another 3.5%, to about $180.3 million. It also leans on $76.2 million in state Transitional Aid, up from $25 million in 2023, according to the state. The state has also run the police since March 27, 2023, when Attorney General Matthew Platkin cited "a crisis of confidence in law enforcement in this city," according to WHYY.

Now what people walk past. Start with crime, fairly. According to the Jersey Vindicator, total crime through Aug. 30 was down 16% from last year. Murders fell from 28 in 2022 to 11 in 2024, then rose to a preliminary 16 in 2025, according to state CompStat reports. Crime overall is down, and I won't say otherwise. But the Vindicator also reported that theft and sexual assault rose this year, and CompStat shows theft up 3.6% in 2025.

Here is what I believe, and it is only my belief. I believe fear of the current administration is the cause of Paterson's lower crime rates, because people know federal sentencing is harsher now. A February 2025 Justice Department memo does tell prosecutors to charge the most serious, readily provable offense, but I have no evidence tying that to Paterson's numbers. I also believe crime is worse than the statistics show, because you can walk through Paterson and see crimes every day that don't get reported. The National Crime Victimization Survey supports part of that: only about 48% of violent crimes and 30% of property crimes nationwide were reported to police in 2024 (Bureau of Justice Statistics).

Homelessness is the clearest thing people see. According to Monarch Housing's NJCounts, Paterson had 140 unsheltered people in 2025, up from 76 in 2024. New federal rules count some seated shelter spaces as unsheltered, and Paterson's total fell from 411 to 336, so I can't say it is simply growing.

Then there are the drugs. You can walk through Paterson and see all kinds of drugs being used in the open: needles in the street, people leaning, high on all kinds of drugs, and people overdosing on the sidewalk. The numbers show where it is happening, even if they don't show it growing. According to the state Department of Health, Passaic County had 154 fatal overdoses in 2023, down from 167 in 2022, but Paterson was one of four towns with 83.8% of those deaths.

Now my opinion, and it is only that. According to the state's higher education profiles, Passaic County Community College's fall undergraduate headcount fell from 5,165 in 2023 to 4,782 in 2024, down 7.4%. The same profiles show that retention of full-time, first-time students fell from 65.6% (Fall 2022 to Fall 2023) to 60.0% (Fall 2023 to Fall 2024). I believe one cause is that students have to walk past homelessness, drugs and crime every day on the way to class. Why keep paying tuition for that? I cannot prove it, and I found no data on why students leave. PCCC's audited statements also cite a demographic decline, and report credit hours up 2.44% in FY2025.

Mayor Sayegh won four more years. I want him to tell residents what their rising taxes are buying, and tell students why downtown will be different.
