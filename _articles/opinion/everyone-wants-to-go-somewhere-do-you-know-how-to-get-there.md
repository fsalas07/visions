Ask almost any student where they want to be in ten years and they'll have an answer. A career, a house, a life that feels like theirs. Now ask how they plan to get there, and the answer gets quiet. Most people know the destination. Very few know the road. I know where I'm going, and I have a plan, at least so far. I want to work on New Jersey's energy grid and the AI infrastructure it will have to power, and I want to try to build an aerospace and energy company. The point is to help power an infrastructure that frees humanity to explore what they really want to do, their purpose and their reason for being in this universe. My plan is to use every resource and everything I'm involved in to get there.

I didn't grow up with a map, and I never found one. I found open doors. I'll keep the details private, but my story includes losing everything and being homeless, which is where growing up the way I did eventually led. I'm not telling you that to ask for sympathy. I'm telling you because it makes one thing very clear to me: the opportunities are already here, and most people walk past them every day.

I started at PCCC in the summer of 2026, and in the months since, my life has been shaped by things that were available to every student on campus. EOF gave me a head start with two credits and the chance to learn my way around campus, and all the opportunities on it, early. I joined Phi Theta Kappa, and in October I traveled with our chapter, Alpha Eta Chi, to the Middle States Region Fall Leadership Conference at Delaware Technical Community College, where our chapter received a Certificate of Recognition. It meant more to me than anyone in that room could have known. Two years ago I was in a shelter eating at Eva's Soup Kitchen, the local soup kitchen. Now I was at a Middle States Region conference, representing PTK as a provisional member, on a trip to Delaware. That didn't happen by luck. It happened because I took advantage of every opportunity thrown at me. I became opinions editor of Visions, which means students can read a view they didn't know they could say out loud. And I'm now president of the B2B STEM Club, where we hold weekly meetings, career workshops, and events for students who want a path in science and technology, or who simply want to build leadership, communication, and networking skills.

None of this required connections or money. It required showing up. A club meeting costs nothing. A conference is a seat on a van. A student newspaper needs writers, and it will take anyone willing to meet a Friday deadline (even though I missed this one).

Here's the problem. The opportunities at a community college don't announce themselves. Nobody sends you a notice that you can lead a club, publish a column, or stand in a room with students from across the region. You find out only if you ask, join, and walk in. Too many students treat community college as a place to take classes and leave, when it is closer to a launchpad with the doors already open.

People are sold a story about four-year colleges. They cost more, and they're more institutionalized, more robotic. You're there for a purpose, and then you're in and you're out. There's very little sense of community, because everyone is on a mission.

Somewhere along the way we forgot what a community college is for. It is not a stop on the way to somewhere else. It is a community that feeds the people who feed it. You give to it, it gives back, and everyone grows.

I don't have a map to give you, only the first steps, and the rest you'll find by walking. This week, walk through one open door. Go to a club meeting. Sit down with an advisor. Visit the tutoring center. Write for the paper. Join an honor society. Every door I walked through was open to you too, and none of them asked me to be ready, only to show up. Don't wait for a perfect plan, because there isn't one. Take one step, then another, and let the path appear as you walk it. The version of you that waits for permission never arrives, and ambition without action is just a wish.

You can't reach a destination you've never stepped toward. The opportunities are right in front of you, so the question becomes whether you're willing to take them.
