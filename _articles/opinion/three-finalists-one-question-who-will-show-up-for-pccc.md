Sometime in November, Passaic County Community College will announce its next president. The Board of Trustees met in closed session on October 29 to discuss the three finalists, and no decision has been made public yet. Whoever is chosen is expected to start in January 2027, ending a search that began after the death of Dr. Steven M. Rose, who led PCCC for decades.

Out of 78 applicants, the Board narrowed the field to three: Dr. Sanjay Rai, Dr. Chioma Ugochukwu and Dr. Donald Guy Generals. Each spent a day on campus from October 26 to 28 and held a public forum in the College Theater. I attended the forums. Here is what I saw.

## Dr. Sanjay Rai: the administrator

Dr. Rai, Maryland's former Secretary of Higher Education, came across as a bureaucrat. Nothing he said was thought-provoking, and I didn't hear him offer real solutions to the college's biggest problems: enrollment, student engagement and the city this college calls home. He has an impressive résumé, but PCCC doesn't need someone who manages problems from behind a desk. It needs someone who solves them.

## Dr. Chioma Ugochukwu: the student advocate in the classroom

Dr. Ugochukwu was the most student-oriented of the three. I believe she is genuinely passionate about students and what they need. But a community college president has to advocate for students outside the classroom too. Our students walk through a city facing crime, drugs and homelessness every day. I'm not convinced she understands that the job reaches past campus walls and into Paterson's streets.

## Dr. Donald Guy Generals: the one who said he'd show up

Dr. Generals said specifically that he would talk to the mayor about the city's problems, and he said something that stuck with me: sometimes you just need to show up, because you can't wait. He also stressed bringing AI onto our campuses, which our students will need for the jobs ahead. Those were the right things to say. Still, I left with the sense that he wanted to do these things for the accolades as much as for the students.

## What PCCC actually needs

A community college president needs to be smart and competent, but that alone isn't enough. They have to understand that they don't only represent students in the classroom. They represent us outside it too: on the walk to the bus stop, in the neighborhoods we live in, and at the table with the people who run this city. Whoever the Board chooses, that is the standard students should hold them to.
