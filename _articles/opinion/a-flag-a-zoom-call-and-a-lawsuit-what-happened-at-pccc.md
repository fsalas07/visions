## The Facts

A former PCCC student is suing the college.

Justin Caiazzo of Lake Hiawatha, a part of Parsippany, filed the lawsuit on Sept. 23, 2026 in New Jersey Superior Court in Morris County. It names Passaic County Community College, Professor Gladys Harris, Dean Peter Hynes and others who are not named.

The case is about a Business Statistics class held on Zoom in July 2021.

PCCC is a public college. That matters because public colleges have to follow the First Amendment.

PCCC representatives were not immediately available for comment, according to Patch. Harris has not spoken publicly either. A judge has not ruled on anything yet.

## What the Lawsuit Claims

Everything in this section comes from Caiazzo's lawsuit. None of it has been proven in court, and the other side has not given its account.

The lawsuit alleges that Caiazzo joined class from home with a "Trump 2020" flag on the wall behind him. Caiazzo claims he never said anything about his politics in class. The flag was just there.

The lawsuit alleges that Harris stopped the class, called the flag "offensive" and said class would not go on until he took it down. When he refused, the lawsuit alleges, she called him "racist" in front of the class and removed him from the Zoom.

Caiazzo claims that at the next class, on July 13, Harris ignored him. When he asked for help with the work, the lawsuit says she told him to "go on tutor.com."

Caiazzo claims he took his complaint to administrators, including Hynes. He says his main worry was that Harris could not grade him fairly after calling him racist. According to the lawsuit, the college sided with Harris and told him to "remain in the class or take an F."

He withdrew and paid for it himself. Caiazzo says he retook the course at William Paterson University and got an 88. He also claims PCCC still has not given him his associate's degree, even though he finished the requirements.

The lawsuit claims free speech violations, viewpoint discrimination, retaliation, First Amendment and due process violations, and negligence. Caiazzo wants money damages, a court order and his lawyer's fees paid.

Harris and PCCC may tell a very different story. So far, they have not told any story in public.

## My Opinion

I believe in the First Amendment no matter what.

In America, every citizen has a right to express their opinion about the government that governs them. That goes for whether they love or hate the administration in power. A Trump flag, a Biden flag, a protest sign, it doesn't matter. If this had been a flag for the other side, it would be just as wrong to throw a student out of class for it.

The Supreme Court settled this a long time ago. In Tinker v. Des Moines in 1969, students wore black armbands to protest the Vietnam War. They didn't say a word. The Court said that kind of silent symbol is protected unless it actually disrupts the school. A flag hanging on a wall at home, on a Zoom call, is about as silent as it gets.

And in Papish v. Board of Curators in 1973, the Court said a public college can't punish a student just because someone finds their speech offensive. Offensive is not the same as disruptive. Being offended is part of living in a free country.

PCCC is a public college. It runs on public money, and it has to follow the Constitution.

That is why this case matters to me. If the lawsuit's claims are true, a public employee paid by taxpayers punished a student for a political symbol. That is especially a problem when someone who receives a taxpayer-funded salary has a problem with the country's Constitution. Public employees don't get to pick which opinions are allowed in their classroom.

The court still has to decide what really happened, and Harris and PCCC deserve to have their side heard. But the principle shouldn't need a court case.

Diminishing the First Amendment has always been the first step to tyranny. Controlling one's speech is trying to control one's thoughts.

A classroom should be the last place in America where that starts.
