PATERSON, NEW JERSEY — Passaic County Community College (PCCC) has appointed Dr. Donald Guy Generals as its sixth president, bringing back an administrator whose professional career in higher education began at the same institution more than four decades ago.

The appointment was announced on November 18, 2026, following a November 17 meeting of the college’s Board of Trustees. Generals is scheduled to officially assume the presidency on January 1, 2027.

A native of Paterson, Generals has held academic and executive leadership positions at several institutions in New Jersey, New York and Pennsylvania. His professional background includes teaching, student affairs, academic administration, institutional planning, financial management and higher education governance.

## His beginnings at Passaic County Community College

According to his professional résumé, Generals began working at Passaic County Community College in 1984. His initial experience included academic support and tutoring.

A profile published by Rutgers University’s Graduate School of Education explains that after completing his undergraduate degree in political science, Generals accepted a tutoring position in PCCC’s learning laboratory. The experience introduced him to the community college system and influenced his decision to pursue a career in higher education.

Between 1990 and 2000, he taught developmental reading and introductory political science at PCCC.

From 1995 to 2000, he served as Dean of Student and Cultural Affairs. In that position, he worked on student development initiatives, academic support, institutional assessment and the establishment of athletic programs. He also served as president of the college’s Academic Council.

His employment at PCCC concluded in 2000, when he moved into senior administrative positions at other institutions.

## Professional career in higher education

After leaving PCCC, Generals joined Rockland Community College in Suffern, New York, where he served as Vice President for Academic and Student Affairs from 2000 to 2003.

During that period, he participated in the institution’s academic restructuring, enrollment management and development of new degree programs. He also served as acting president during the president’s absences.

In November 2003, he became Provost of Katharine Gibbs School in New York City, a position he held until June 2008. His responsibilities included overseeing academic programs, faculty and the development of associate degree and certificate offerings.

His next position was Vice President for Academic Affairs at Mercer County Community College in West Windsor, New Jersey, where he served from 2008 to 2014.

At Mercer, Generals participated in the development of the institution’s Educational Master Plan, expanded academic programming and helped establish its study-abroad program. His work also included curriculum development, improvements to developmental education and English as a Second Language programs, and capital projects at the James Kerney Campus in Trenton.

He also participated in the implementation of the American Honors Program, an initiative designed to provide academically motivated students with additional opportunities to pursue admission to four-year universities.

## Eleven years leading the Community College of Philadelphia

In March 2014, the Board of Trustees of the Community College of Philadelphia announced Generals’ appointment as its sixth president. He officially began his presidency on July 1, 2014, and remained in the position through June 2025.

During his 11-year tenure, he served as president and chief executive officer of Philadelphia’s public community college.

His résumé documents responsibility for an annual institutional budget exceeding $250 million, more than 1,200 employees and over 30,000 credit and noncredit students across four campuses.

Among the initiatives documented in his professional record were the establishment of the Octavius Catto Scholarship Fund, the implementation of Guided Pathways, and the creation of a middle college program that allows eligible high school students to pursue an associate degree while completing their high school education.

His résumé also reports that he oversaw approximately $100 million in capital projects, including the development of an advanced technology center and renovations to campus facilities.

In financial administration, his résumé records 11 consecutive balanced budgets, 10 years of clean audits, an A3 stable bond rating and an institutional fund balance of $80 million. It also reports that his fundraising efforts secured more than $50 million in public and private donations during the private phase of a comprehensive campaign.

These figures are reported in his published professional résumé.

## Academic qualifications and research

Generals’ academic background includes degrees from two New Jersey institutions.

He earned a Bachelor of Arts in Political Science from William Paterson College in 1983 and a master’s degree in Urban Education from the same institution in 1988.

In 1998, he completed a Doctor of Education (Ed.D.) in Social and Philosophical Foundations of Education at Rutgers University in New Brunswick.

He also completed executive education programs at Harvard University’s Institute for Educational Management and Cornell University’s Interest-Based Bargaining program.

In 2024, Holy Family University awarded him an honorary Doctorate of Humane Letters.

His academic work has focused on education, institutional leadership and the history of progressive education in the United States.

In 2013, he published *Booker T. Washington: The Architect of Progressive Education*, a book developed from his doctoral research examining the educational contributions and ideas of Booker T. Washington.

His professional résumé also lists publications in the *Journal of Negro Education*, *Diverse Issues in Higher Education*, *Community College Week* and the *Philadelphia Tribune*.

## Professional recognition and community involvement

Throughout his career, Generals has participated in professional associations and community organizations connected to higher education and regional development.

His résumé documents service on the executive committee of the American Association of Community Colleges from 2023 to 2024 and participation in the National Alliance of Community and Technical Colleges, the College Board’s Middle States Region and the Global Community College Leadership Network.

His community service includes previous membership on the Paterson Board of Education from 2001 to 2003.

His listed recognitions include the 2022 Globy Award for Educational Leadership from Global Philadelphia, the National Pacesetter of the Year Award in 2021 from the National Council for Marketing and Public Relations, and the Community Leadership Award from the Urban Affairs Coalition in 2024.

He was also included in Philadelphia Magazine’s 150 Most Influential list in 2022, 2023 and 2024, according to his résumé.

## The presidential selection process at PCCC

The selection of Generals followed a national search initiated in 2026 after the passing of Dr. Steven M. Rose, who served as president of Passaic County Community College for nearly three decades.

The Board of Trustees partnered with the Association of Community College Trustees (ACCT) Searches to coordinate the recruitment and selection process.

On October 14, 2026, the college announced three finalists: Donald Guy Generals, Dr. Sanjay Rai and Dr. Chioma Ugochukwu.

The finalists participated in public forums on campus on October 26, 27 and 28, respectively. Faculty, staff, students and members of the public were invited to attend and submit feedback to the Board of Trustees.

Following the selection process, the Board announced Generals as the incoming president during its November 17 meeting.

## A return to the institution where his career began

Generals’ appointment establishes a professional connection between his previous experience at PCCC and his forthcoming role as the institution’s chief executive.

After beginning his career at the college in 1984, he spent the following decades teaching, managing academic programs and leading higher education institutions in three states.

He will officially become PCCC’s sixth president on January 1, 2027.

His appointment places an administrator with previous experience at the institution at the head of a college he first joined as an academic support professional more than 40 years ago.
